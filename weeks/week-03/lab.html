Artificial Intelligence Applications in Engineering (MUH-920), week 3 of 14: interactive lab

Rule-based reasoning lab: Soil classification and pump diagnosis

Prof. Dr. Utku Kose, Süleyman Demirel University

Part A classifies a soil with the Unified Soil Classification System from grain-size and plasticity values set with sliders, and prints the reasoning path rule by rule next to the plasticity chart [1, 2]. Part B is a diagnostic expert system for a centrifugal pump with certainty factors: Symptoms are selected with a confidence, rules fire forward, evidence combines as in MYCIN, and every conclusion can be asked how it was reached [3]. Part C runs backward chaining on the pump rule base of the lecture and answers the questions why and how. Part D evaluates every certainty-factor formula of the lecture step by step, and Part E computes the grading curve, D10, D30, D60, Cu and Cc from the masses of a sieve analysis and completes the group symbol [2, 3, 4].

Part A: USCS classification with an explanation

    Part B: Pump diagnosis with certainty factors

    Set a certainty for each observation (0 means not observed). Conclusions and their combined certainty update immediately. Select a conclusion to see how it was reached.

    ConclusionCertaintyRecommended action

    Part C: Backward chaining that answers why and how

    Choose a goal, a fault or a maintenance action, and answer the questions of the engine. It works backward from the goal through the rules of the lecture, asks only about the observations that the goal needs, and records each subgoal in an indented trace. The button Why names the rule that the open question serves, and the button How lists the chain of rules behind a conclusion [3, 4].

    Trace of the engine

    Part D: The algebra of certainty factors

    The first panel passes certainty from a premise to a conclusion with the minimum or maximum of the conditions, the threshold of 0.2 and the rule certainty. The second panel combines two certainty factors with the formula chosen by their signs, in both orders, and plots the result for every value of the second one. The third panel combines any sequence of rule outputs; its default is the worked example of the lecture [3, 5].

    Part E: From sieve masses to a group symbol

    Enter the masses retained on each sieve and the Atterberg limits of the fines. The lab computes the percentages passing, draws the grading curve on a logarithmic scale, interpolates D10, D30 and D60 with every step shown, and applies the rules of the Unified Soil Classification System; the defaults are the sand of the worked example [1, 2].

    Six questions with instant feedback. Rate your confidence before checking each answer.

    Answers are saved in this browser only. The export creates a Markdown learning log for your portfolio.

    After the export, continue with the discipline challenge and the weekly task in the week overview.
