Regression studio: Complexity, validation and leakage

Prof. Dr. Utku Kose, Süleyman Demirel University

Course homeWeek overviewLecture pageColab notebookPDF notes

Part A generates strength data from a known law, shaped after Abrams' water-cement rule [1], adds noise, and lets polynomial, nearest-neighbour and tree regressors fit it; training error, error on new data and a five-fold validation curve show where overfitting starts [2]. Part B is a leakage detective with six candidate features from engineering projects [3]. Part C fits a least-squares line by hand and shows how ridge and the lasso shrink it. Part D computes MAE, RMSE and R² term by term with a parity plot, Part E compares nearest neighbours with and without standardised inputs, Part F searches tree splits and adds gradient-boosting stumps, and Part G estimates bias and variance by repeated sampling [2, 7, 9, 10].

Part A: Fit, validate, compare

Five-fold validation curve over the complexity setting of the chosen model. The dashed line marks the current setting.

Part B: Leakage detective

Part C: Least squares, ridge and lasso by hand

The table computes every deviation, product and square of the least-squares formulas for the pairs entered above, and the chart draws the line with its residuals. The two sliders shrink the slope with the ridge and lasso formulas of the lecture; the lasso slope becomes exactly zero once λ reaches twice the magnitude of S_xy. The defaults are the five mixes of the worked example [4, 6].

Part D: Error metrics and the parity plot

Measured and predicted values give MAE, RMSE and R² with every term shown. Adding one large error raises RMSE much more than MAE and lowers R², and the parity plot shows the point far from the diagonal [7].

Part E: Nearest neighbours with and without scaling

Six mixes with a water-cement ratio, an age and a strength form the training data of the worked example. Moving the query shows the raw and the standardised distances side by side; in raw units the age decides alone, and after standardisation both inputs count [8].

Part F: Tree splits and gradient-boosting stumps

The table evaluates every candidate threshold of a regression tree with the summed squared error of its two children and marks the best one. Each press of the button fits a one-split tree to the current residuals and adds it, scaled by the learning rate, so that the boosted model approaches the data step by step [9, 10].

Part G: Bias and variance by repeated sampling

Two hundred training sets are drawn from the same law with noise, and a polynomial is fitted to each. At the point x0, the mean of the predictions gives the bias and their spread the variance; the second chart shows how the two terms trade off over the degree, as in the decomposition of the lecture [2].
