Convolution explorer: Filters, feature maps and receptive fields

Prof. Dr. Utku Kose, Süleyman Demirel University

Course homeWeek overviewLecture pageColab notebookPDF notes

Part A lets a greyscale image be drawn or generated, applies a chosen or hand-edited 3 by 3 filter, and shows the feature map, its ReLU and a 2 by 2 max pooling, which are the three steps of a convolutional block [1]. Part B computes such a block product by product, and Part C computes output sizes, parameter counts and receptive fields for a stack of convolution and pooling layers. Part D produces the eight mirrored and rotated versions of an image, and Part E computes the softmax, the cross-entropy and the gradients of a new classifier head [2]. Part F measures boxes, suppression and segmentation masks [3, 4], Part G computes a Grad-CAM heatmap by hand [5], and Part H shows how a perturbation too small to see flips a linear detector [6].

Part B: A convolutional block number by number

The default input is the 4 by 4 patch of the worked example with the filter for dark vertical lines and the bias −0.5. Every value of the input and of the filter can be edited. Clicking a value of the feature map writes out the nine products of its window, the bias, the ReLU and the pooling window that contains it, and padding or a stride of 2 shows how the output size changes [1].

Part D: Eight versions of one image

An image is rotated by quarter turns, with and without mirroring, which gives the eight versions used for augmentation in the notebook. A clicked pixel is followed through all versions with the formula for its new position, and a ninth panel changes contrast and brightness with clipping [7].

Click a pixel of the original to follow it through the eight versions.

Part E: Softmax and a new classifier head

A new head of two classes receives three features. The panel computes the scores, the exponentials, the softmax probabilities and the cross-entropy, then the gradients of the scores, weights and biases, and applies a gradient step; the defaults reproduce the scores 0.4 and 1.9 of the worked example. The second panel counts the parameters of a frozen network with a new head [2, 8].

Frozen network and new head

Part F: Boxes, suppression and masks

The first panel computes the intersection over union of a true and a predicted box, the second runs non-maximum suppression on a list of boxes with confidences, and the third compares a predicted mask with the true mask and measures the area, length and mean width of a crack; the length is taken as the number of rows the crack spans, which suits cracks that run from top to bottom [3, 4].

Click a cell to cycle through background, true crack only, predicted crack only, and both.

Part G: Grad-CAM by hand

The maps and gradients of the worked example are the default. The panel averages the gradients of every map into its weight, sums the weighted maps, applies ReLU and normalises, and lays the enlarged heatmap over an image of a crack. The second preset adds a map that fires on a timestamp in the corner and receives a large gradient, so the heatmap reveals a classifier that is right for the wrong reason [5].

Part H: A perturbation too small to see

A linear detector scores an image of a crack with weights of equal size, negative on the band where it expects the crack and positive elsewhere. Each pixel changes by ε against the sign of its weight. The perturbation is invisible in the perturbed image, yet the score falls by ε times the sum of the weight sizes, as in the worked example [6].
